# English proofing

Polish the wording. Leave the claims, the boundaries, and the examples where they are.

Return the revised English as the deliverable. Do not open with a commentary on technique. If the user asked in Chinese, the revised passage stays in English. Add a short note afterward only for smoother wordings you refused because they would change what the passage includes or excludes. If nothing was refused, stop after the revised text.

## Do not change

- What a category, claim, or instruction includes and excludes
- Examples, numbers, names, and JSON or form fields
- A technical term, when the everyday substitute is wider or narrower

`subject` → `topic`, `Reflexive Self` → `Self`, and "projects into a future scenario" → "outside the current context" are meaning changes, not proofing.

If a grammatical fix and a meaning change are stuck in the same sentence, fix the grammar and keep the original boundary.

## Moves

### 1. Cut notes the reader does not execute

Delete a heading or line that records the author's own process (`Task1`, "read 631 comments and generate this codebook") when the rest of the text is already the instruction. If that line is the task, keep it and only fix its English.

### 2. Short names, one frame for siblings

Use this when the text has labels, categories, or a paired contrast.

Drop a word from a label only when the definition still states the same test (`Robot itself` → `Robot` if the definition still lists appearance, movement, performance, and status). Sibling items share one frame:

- `Other People in Videos` / `Other People in Comments`
- `Event/Competition` → `Event or Competition`

Write `or`, not a slash.

### 3. Open a definition on the thing being defined

The first words name the unit: `Comments regarding…`, `First-person comments that…`. Do not open on an inner process (`Projects the robot into…`) when the reader still has to infer what kind of item this is.

### 4. One complete sentence

Unstick fused clauses (`capabilitiesimagining`). One main clause states the existing boundary. Use `including` for a single example already in the original. Do not replace a stack of conditions with a new, wider condition.

### 5. Point at the thing

Replace a noun that can mean two things with the one the definition already names (`content` / `Footage` → `video`, when the definition says people visible in the video). Use that same word in the definition and in the rule.

### 6. Everyday word only as an alias of the same test

`Comment Section` → `comments` names the same place. `coding assistant` → `research assistant` does not: here `coding` means qualitative coding. Keep the method term unless the user asked to drop it.

### 7. Name the mix-up, then use the labels you just defined

`Remember the distinction between "Other People in Videos" and "Other People in Comments":` then one clause each. Do not invent a third name in the rule (`Footage`, `Others in the video`) after the definitions have been renamed.

### 8. A slot label is a noun phrase

`Briefly explain the reason` → `Brief explanation` when the line labels a field. Keep a full instruction when the line is the command.

## Last pass

Read the whole piece once. The same thing has one name everywhere. No leftover merges (`topicsubject`, `orCompetition`, `includingl`, `commnets`).

## Worked split

Original:

> Projects the robot into a future scenario or assigns it a role beyond its current capabilitiesimagining future and life with a robot and beyond the current marathon context.

Proofing keeps the boundary and makes one sentence:

> Comments that project the robot into a future scenario, or assign it a role beyond its current capabilities, including a future life with a robot beyond this marathon.

This is not proofing. The boundary moved:

> Comments about robots outside the current marathon context, including future applications.

The second sentence drops role-assignment and "life with a robot," and lets in any robot talk outside the race.
