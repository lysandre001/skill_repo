# English writing

Someone hands you a passage that is already in English and still hard to read. The words are correct enough. The sentence makes you stop, go back, and put the pieces together yourself. Rewrite it so a reader can hear it the first time.

Give back the rewritten passage. Don't start by explaining how you rewrote it. If they asked in Chinese, what you return is still English.

## Read it aloud

The places you stumble are the places to rewrite.

A name that explains itself is hard to say. "Robot itself", "Reflexive Self", "Others in the Comment Section". Say the short name, and name neighboring things the same way: other people in the video, other people in the comments. Write "event or competition", not "event/competition".

A definition that begins in the middle of an action makes the reader ask what is being defined. "Projects the robot into a future scenario" starts too late. Begin with the thing:

> Comments that project the robot into a future scenario, or give it a role beyond what it can do now, including a life with a robot after this marathon.

That sentence can be said in one breath. The stiff version had glued the next thought on until two words fused: "capabilitiesimagining". Separate them. Keep what the writer was actually saying. A smoother line that means something else is a different passage, not a better one. "Comments about robots outside the marathon, including future uses" is easy to read, and it no longer says projecting, or a role, or a life with a robot.

A word you cannot point at will be read two ways. "Content" might be the video or the comment. The next paragraph calls the same thing "footage". Choose the word a person would say, video or comments, and keep saying it.

When two things are easy to mix up, say so the way you would say it to someone next to you. "People in the video are the ones on screen. People in the comments are the ones writing to each other." Don't send the reader back through "the former" and "the latter", and don't give the same thing a new name in the reminder.

A label on a blank wants a name. "Brief explanation" sits on the line more quietly than "Briefly explain the reason."

A note the writer left for themselves is not part of the piece. "Task 1. Read through 631 comments and generate this codebook" is a note on the desk. Unless they asked you to keep their working notes, leave it out.

## A paragraph, not only a list

In a stretch of prose, do the same thing one sentence at a time. Put the person or the thing first. Let the sentence end where a speaker would breathe, then start the next one. Replace "regarding", "in terms of", and "the former" with the verb you mean. If a phrase only repeats the phrase before it, cut it. Then read the paragraph again. If every sentence has the same shape, change one of them so the ear can rest.

## Before and after

Stiff:

> Others in the content: comments regarding people visible in the video, such as engineers, programmers, security guards, audience members.
>
> Distinction between "Others in the Footage" and "Others in the Comment Section": The former refers to people appearing in the video; the latter refers to users interacting in the comment section.

Fluent:

> Other people in the video: comments about people you can see, such as engineers, programmers, security guards, or people in the audience.
>
> Remember the difference. People in the video are the ones on screen. People in the comments are the ones writing to each other.

Nothing new was added. Engineers, guards, and the audience are still there. The second version is only easier to read.
